Academic Theory & Models Guide

Understand the mathematics and assumptions behind the pricing engines.

Black-Scholes Model

The foundational continuous-time model developed by Fischer Black, Myron Scholes, and Robert Merton (1973).

  • Assumptions: Log-normal distribution of stock prices, constant risk-free rate, constant volatility, no transaction costs, continuous trading.
  • Best for: European options with known parameters. Fast, analytical exact solution.
  • Limitation: Cannot accurately price American options before expiration or handle discrete dividends easily.

Binomial Tree (CRR Model)

A discrete-time model developed by Cox, Ross, and Rubinstein (1979).

  • Mechanism: Builds a recombining tree of possible future stock prices (Up or Down movements) out to expiration, then works backward to find today's price.
  • Best for: American options, as early exercise can be evaluated at every tree node.
  • Convergence: As the number of steps approaches infinity, the Binomial price converges to the Black-Scholes price.

Monte Carlo Simulation

A stochastic method that simulates thousands of possible future price paths.

  • Mechanism: Uses Geometric Brownian Motion (GBM) to randomly walk the stock price forward. The option payoff is averaged across all paths and discounted to the present.
  • Variance Reduction: This application uses Antithetic Variates, computing paths in pairs (positive shock and negative shock) to reduce standard error without doubling computation time.
  • Best for: Highly complex, path-dependent exotic options (like Asians or Barriers).

The Greeks

First-order and second-order derivatives of the pricing formula.

  • Delta (Δ): First derivative w.r.t Spot Price. Represents hedge ratio.
  • Gamma (Γ): Second derivative w.r.t Spot Price. Measures convexity and delta stability.
  • Theta (Θ): First derivative w.r.t Time. Represents daily time decay. Always negative for long options.
  • Vega (ν): First derivative w.r.t Volatility. Highest when At-The-Money.
  • Rho (ρ): First derivative w.r.t Risk-Free Rate. Generally least impactful for short-term retail trading, but critical for long-dated LEAPS.

Welcome to the Builder

The Strategy Builder is a multi-panel trading laboratory for designing, testing, and comparing options strategies before you risk real money.

Who is this for? Anyone designing a multi-leg options trade — from a simple long call to iron condors and butterflies.

This guide walks through each panel in order. Use the arrows below to navigate.

🌐

Panel 0 · Market Context Bar

The horizontal bar at the top holds the market assumptions that every leg inherits. Set it once and the entire builder recomputes instantly.

  • Ticker — cosmetic label only (e.g., "AAPL")
  • Spot (S) — current underlying price
  • Volatility (σ) — implied vol in %
  • Expiration — auto-computes T in years
  • Rate (r) / Yield (q) — risk-free & dividend rates
⚠️ When NOT to use: Do not set Spot to a value wildly different from realistic prices — all P/L math anchors off this. An unrealistic spot makes every downstream panel meaningless.
🎛️

Panel 1 · Strategy Leg Manager

Each "leg" is one option contract in your strategy. Add, remove, and tweak legs here.

  • Buy / Sell — paying premium vs. collecting premium
  • Call / Put / Stock — the instrument type
  • Strike (K) — the anchor price for the contract
  • Qty — number of contracts (×100 shares each)
Straddle: Buy 1 Call + Buy 1 Put @ same strike
Strangle: Buy 1 Call (high K) + Buy 1 Put (low K)
Bull Spread: Buy low K Call + Sell high K Call
Iron Condor: Sell OTM Call spread + Sell OTM Put spread
📈

Panel 2 · Multi-Line P/L Chart

Shows your Net P/L across a ±30% spot range at five different moments in time:

  • ■ Today — value right now
  • ■ 75% time left
  • ■ 50% time left
  • ■ 25% time left
  • ■ At Expiration — the classic hockey-stick payoff
Use this to: Spot convergence between lines (when time value erodes), identify theta decay visually, and find your breakeven range (where lines cross the zero dashed line).
🔍

Reading the P/L Chart

  • The white dashed line = Break-even at $0 P/L
  • Lines above the dashed line = you're profitable
  • Lines below = you're at a loss
  • The gap between "Today" and "Expiration" lines shows the time value you still have
  • If lines converge quickly, your strategy has high negative theta (costs you every day)
⚠️ When NOT to rely on this chart: If your expiration date is less than 3 days away, BS pricing becomes unreliable near-ATM. Use intrinsic value instead.
⚗️

Panel 3 · Net Greeks Summary

Five cards showing the aggregate sensitivity of your whole strategy — not just one leg.

  • Net Δ (Delta) — directional exposure per $1 move in spot. ~0 means delta-neutral.
  • Net Γ (Gamma) — how fast delta changes. Positive = accelerating gains when you move.
  • Net ν (Vega) — P/L change per 1% vol move. Positive = you profit from vol spikes.
  • Net Θ (Theta) / day — daily time decay. Negative = the clock costs you money.
  • Net ρ (Rho) — sensitivity to interest rate changes. Usually minor.
Straddle check: Net Δ ≈ 0, Γ > 0, Vega > 0, Theta < 0. If yours shows this, the math is correct.
🧮

Panel 4 · Net Greeks vs Spot Chart

Shows how each Greek evolves as spot moves across the ±30% range. Click a label in the legend to toggle individual Greeks on/off.

  • Use it to find where delta flips sign (directional exposure reversal)
  • A hump-shaped Gamma curve = maximum convexity near the strikes
  • Vega peaking near ATM is expected — vol matters most there
⚠️ When NOT to use: Greeks vs Spot is theoretical — it assumes all other parameters stay fixed. In reality, vol changes as spot moves (skew). This chart ignores that.
📊

Summary Cards

The three cards below the chart give you a quick read of your strategy's expiration profile.

  • Max Profit — best case. Shows ♾️ if uncapped (e.g., long call).
  • Max Loss — worst case. Shows ⚠️ if uncapped (e.g., short call).
  • Breakevens — exact prices where you neither profit nor lose at expiration.
Use case: Before entering any trade, confirm your max loss is a number you can comfortably absorb. Never enter if max loss says "Unlimited" unless you truly understand short options risk.
🧪

Panel 5 · Scenario Sandbox

A completely isolated stress-testing environment — moves here do NOT affect the main chart. Think of it as a "what if" calculator.

  • Spot Change (%) — simulate a ±30% price move without touching Panel 0
  • IV Change (pts) — model a volatility expansion or crush
  • Days Elapsed — fast-forward time to see theta bleed
Use case: Earnings plays — simulate a +15% gap-up AND a vol crush (-20 pts) simultaneously to see if your straddle actually profits.
⚡

Scenario Presets & Saved Scenarios

Three one-click presets apply common real-world shocks:

Bull Case: +10% spot, -5 IV pts, 3 days — stock rallied and vol dropped
Bear Case: -10% spot, +5 IV pts, 3 days — sell-off with vol spike
Theta Bleed: 0% spot, 0 IV, 7 days — one week passes with nothing happening

Click "Save Scenario" to snapshot any slider state into the comparison table below (max 6). Perfect for showing teammates different outcomes side-by-side.

⚠️ When NOT to use: Saved scenarios are not persistent — they reset if you refresh the page. Screenshot or export before closing.
💾

Panel 6 · Save Strategy

Click "Save Strategy" at the top to snapshot your current legs. Up to 4 strategies can be saved at once.

  • A green toast notification confirms the save
  • Saved strategies are named automatically ("Strategy 1", "Strategy 2"…)
  • You cannot edit a saved strategy — modify the builder and save a new snapshot
  • The Load button in the Comparison view restores any saved strategy into the active builder
Use case: Save a Straddle, then modify the builder to a Strangle. Now you can compare the two side-by-side.
⚖️

Panel 7 · Compare Strategies

Once 2 or more strategies are saved, the "Compare" button appears. It opens a full comparison modal with:

  • A side-by-side metrics table: Legs, Net Cost, Max Loss/Gain, Breakevens, all 5 Greeks
  • An Expiration P/L Overlay chart — all strategies on one canvas
  • A Today's P/L Overlay chart — showing current theoretical value curves
⚠️ Key rule: All saved strategies are automatically recomputed using the current Panel 0 market context. Comparisons are only valid if the strategies share the same underlying. Do not compare AAPL strategies to SPY strategies using this tool.
⚠️

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Setting T (time) too small — BS breaks down below ~3 days ATM
  • Ignoring Net Theta — even a good-looking P/L chart can be bleeding $50/day
  • Comparing strategies with different expiration dates — the chart assumes a single T for all legs
  • Using the Scenario Sandbox to change base parameters — it's a sandbox, not the main builder
  • Treating Max Gain = ♾️ as "free money" — uncapped upside comes with uncapped risk on the other side (short premium)
🚀

You're Ready to Build!

Here's the recommended workflow:

  1. Set Panel 0 with your real market parameters
  2. Add legs in Panel 1 to build your strategy
  3. Read Panel 2 to understand the time-value decay curve
  4. Check Panel 3 for Net Delta neutrality and Theta cost
  5. Stress-test in the Scenario Sandbox
  6. Save and compare multiple strategies
  7. Pick the one with the best risk/reward for your view
